**Data:** Lombardi et al., Health Affairs Scholar 2024, Table 1 (BLS SOII data, 2011 to 2021/2022). New BLS data was not available when this report ran.

## Key findings

- Highest latest rate: **Psychiatric and substance abuse hospitals** at 110.4 per 10,000 full-time workers.
- Median change across 12 facility types: +41% (2011-2013 avg vs 2018-2020 avg).
- 11 of 12 facility types rose by 10% or more; 1 stayed about flat.

## Notes

- Rates count injuries with at least one day away from work caused by an intentional injury by another person.
- From 2021-2022 on, BLS publishes two-year periods, and its fact sheets use a broader case definition (days away, job restriction, or transfer). Compare those periods with earlier years carefully.
- Small industries have volatile year-to-year rates; look at averages and multi-year trends.

## Known limits and possible bias

- Undercounting: cases come from employer records; unreported violence and violence without a missed workday are not counted. Reporting differs among staff (younger nurses report more; Friese et al., 2024).
- A rising rate can mean more violence, better reporting, or both.
- Facility types with missing years are left out rather than estimated; results cover only the types shown.
- BLS detailed tables cover private industry; government-run hospitals may not be included.
- The data describes injured workers and settings, not the people who caused harm. High rates in psychiatric and residential settings reflect the care setting, not people with mental illness or disabilities in general.
- Serious event analyses are confidential: data from incident reporting systems related to Serious Event analysis is not used or shared. What happened may be shared; the analysis of what led to it may not. This report uses only public, published data.
